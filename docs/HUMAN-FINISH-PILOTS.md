# Human Finish pilot selection and first-work orders

**Status:** proposed work orders, not observed customer journeys. Recorded 2026-11-10. No production modifications are authorized by this plan. Use read-only inspections, actual customer tasks and real artifacts. The project's local `TESTS-REMOVED.md` records a November 7 order to remove synthetic tests: do not reintroduce self-serving fixture suites as a substitute for product use. The existing `npm run check` is a syntax check only.

## Pilot 1: EzSeller / CommerceOS, MCP decision and professional work output

**External desired result:** An Amazon seller knows which account problem is costing money or blocking sales, who can fix it and how to verify the outcome.  
**Internal research hypothesis:** The operator makes a sequence of judgments across suppression, inventory, reimbursements, settlement data and case follow-up; a raw endpoint/tool list is an inadequate deliverable.

**Study the real work:** replay an authorized, read-only seller exception investigation: trigger -> inventory/listing/finance evidence -> competing hypotheses -> permitted next actions -> report/handoff -> next-day verification. Capture where the operator leaves the MCP to do manual work, what they distrust, why they would pay for the result and what must remain supervised.

**Rejection criteria:** no verifiable source or time window; guessed causal diagnosis; action without permission; no next owner; no useful report; work marked complete without platform-side proof.

**Customer-facing language direction, only after evidence:** describe the resolved commercial problem and the handled service, not the count of MCP tools. Keep true limitations visible at purchase time.

## Pilot 2: CashCrop, decision-support web app / MCP

**Desired result:** A dispensary operator identifies an actionable inventory, purchasing or margin opportunity and can verify a decision before acting.  
**Research hypothesis:** Buyers care less about a polished dashboard than about decisions they can defend to a manager, buyer or owner. The claim requires real customer validation.

**Study the real work:** a recurring replenishment or margin decision with an authorized dataset, held-out contradictory/partial inventory records, approval boundary and export or saved decision. Check exactly what was available when the decision was made, not today's data.

**Rejection criteria:** misleading metric denominator, stale/estimated data presented as current, price/profit claims outrunning actual data, attractive visualization with no decision consequence, agent can't recover from missing integration access.

**Brand question:** Does the product feel like a trusted independent commercial analyst rather than a generic AI dashboard? Evidence and real operator reactions decide the answer.

## Pilot 3: AiSCent, outcome-first editorial marketing

**Desired result:** A developer feels the relief of getting from an almost-finished app to a clearly understood release-ready next step.  
**Standing distinction:** Advertising is never a screenshot demonstration; App Store screenshots must reflect real app functionality. The existing policy `AISCENT-ADS-CUSTOMER-OUTCOME-NONOVERRIDABLE-V1` is non-overridable.

**Study the real buyer:** their exact stalled-release moment, their own words, what uncertainty costs them, what a believable successful outcome looks like, and why this offer earns trust. Compare three distinctive visual narratives, not three palette variations.

**Rejection criteria:** pasted app UI in awareness ads, glamorous imagery unrelated to the actual release situation, product identity drift, unverifiable claims, generic emotional language, no independent first-exposure comprehension evidence.

**Craft:** real original format-specific photography, no fabricated app proof, separately editable localized typography, real native export and human aesthetic review. Reuse existing AiSCent editorial work order and evidence pipeline.

## Pilot 4: Unloopr, service brand / website and product trust

**Desired result:** A developer gets an accurate, usable explanation of an avoidable code failure and a concrete path to correction.  
**Protected identity:** Unloopr is customer-facing; do not rebrand sign-in, checkout or website as SMAT. Preserve the currently authorized technical issuer until migration is separately verified.

**Study the real job:** the exact moment a developer seeks help, what code and logs they can supply, what the tool can *actually* conclude, what resolution means, and what happens if the initial diagnosis is wrong.

**Rejection criteria:** anonymous confidence theater, a generic "AI code quality" pitch, brand/logo replacement, unverified before-and-after claim, absent checkout/recovery path, wrong assumptions about permission and tool access.

**Editorial question:** Show relief from a stuck problem, not the debugging machinery. A technical proof example can appear in evaluation content but must remain real.

## Pilot evidence packet for each

1. Baseline exact source and live URL/command, captured on the authorized host.
2. Recorded real user action and its raw output artifact; redact sensitive information.
3. Source-linked as-done work map and exception list.
4. Per-brand intent charter, including rejected design directions.
5. One prioritized repair plan with observable before/after acceptance.
6. User-facing output or visual before/after and independent human critique.
7. Named unresolved unknowns and explicit HOLD, not automatic approvals.

**Program measure:** Track separately (a) critical-job completion, (b) time/steps left to the human, (c) first-exposure comprehension, (d) recoverability, (e) repeated defects and (f) paid-result fulfillment. Never aggregate these into one invented quality score or convert one favorable session into a conversion claim.
