## 6. Craft and evidence commitments

**P0 stop lines:** false promise, inaccessible critical action, unauthorized use, cross-tenant disclosure, unearned charge, missing actual delivery, misleading provenance.  
**P1 stop lines:** first-time task blocked; lost work; broken error recovery, control or core accessibility.  
**P2 repairs:** legibility, hierarchy, contrast, consistency, responsive/localization defects; independently inspect actual pixels.  
**P3 craft pass:** optical layout, word-level copy, typography, pacing, meaningful microinteractions and durable details.

Evidence to attach: exact candidate SHA, baseline SHA, source-linked customer evidence, changed routes/states, screenshot/trace receipts, relevant donor sources and license, negative fixtures, human visual reviewer, representative first-time sessions, exact-main and live SHA, adverse regression. Existing Interface Lab receipt-gate checks local hashes and declaration shape only. It never authenticates the reviewer, verifies a live service, or declares quality automatically.

## 7. The quality learning loop

For each verified defect, record:

`date | project | job/situation | evidence ID | observed behavior | user consequence | severity | cause | correction | regression fixture | reviewer | recheck date | shareable rule or local exception`

When a defect recurs in two different projects, consider a shared guardrail. Preserve each brand's unique point of view. An old defect is not closed until a fail-before / pass-after test or explicitly labeled human observation is recorded. A changed underlying artifact invalidates its old approval.

## Charter decision

- **Ready for concept work:** HOLD until a real person, job, first value, and brand position are documented.
- **Ready to build:** HOLD until baseline, protected behavior, source/reuse and the target-specific acceptance contract are documented.
- **Ready to release:** use existing `ACCEPTANCE.md` and independent production/customer proof. This template never returns APPROVED.

No completed-looking template may substitute for real observation. When missing evidence is important, **show the gap and design the smallest legitimate study**, rather than guessing.
